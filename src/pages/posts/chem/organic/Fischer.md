# Newman projection and Fischer projection

<img src="/images/2026-9-27.svg" alt="投影式示意图" style="display: block; width: 100%; max-width: 560px; height: auto; margin: 1.5rem auto; background: #fff;">

The above are some examples of problems requiring the drawing of Newman and Fischer projections given a wedge shape. For the Newman projection, simply draw it normally according to the three bonds in the wedge shape. When observing, look from the side of the target carbon. For example, the Newman projections in the above diagram are drawn with the left carbon as the front carbon and viewed from the left. The Newman projection allows an even number of interleavings of groups on the same carbon atom.

For the Fischer projection, you can first draw the Newman projection and then convert it to the Fischer projection. The method is to rotate the already drawn Newman projection to obtain an overlapping structure, with the three groups of the front and back carbons arranged in a Y-shape. If it is already an overlapping structure, no rotation is needed, as shown in the diagram. Then, write the group on the left connected to the back carbon (circumference) to the left of the first horizontal line, and the group on the right connected to the right of the first horizontal line; write the group on the left connected to the front carbon (center) to the left of the second horizontal line, and the group on the right connected to the right of the second horizontal line. Finally, write the end groups that overlap directly below, with the later carbon group at the top of the vertical line and the earlier carbon group at the bottom, thus obtaining the Fischer projection. At this point, it is important to check whether the main chain is on the vertical line. If not, as shown in the structure on the left side of the third row in the figure, the main chain needs to be moved to the vertical line by an even number of pairwise exchanges between groups. The Fischer projection also allows an even number of pairwise exchanges between groups.
